Over the past decade, Lisbon's rental market has undergone a dramatic transformation, and this report sets out to explain why. At the heart of that explanation lies municipal leasing policy.

A more promising avenue for understanding the regulatory dynamics at play is the European Union's directive on home-sharing, which appears to function as the key lever shaping municipal policy in this area. Rather than treating local rules in isolation, framing them as downstream implementations of this EU-level directive offers a more coherent explanatory structure. This shift in emphasis suggests that future analysis should prioritize tracing how the directive's provisions are transposed into local ordinances, rather than treating municipal preferences for long-term leases as a freestanding or self-explanatory phenomenon.
